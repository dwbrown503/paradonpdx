Week 4 · The Cultural Context of Ancient Leadership

Monday – Power That Rules from Above

"Everybody can be great... because anybody can serve." — Martin Luther King Jr. (from his sermon "The Drum Major Instinct," preached February 4, 1968)
Bible (ESV): Mark 10:42-45 — "And Jesus called them to him and said to them, 'You know that those who are considered rulers of the Gentiles lord it over them, and their great ones exercise authority over them. But it shall not be so among you. But whoever would be great among you must be your servant, and whoever would be first among you must be slave of all. For even the Son of Man came not to be served but to serve, and to give his life as a ransom for many.'"

In the ancient world, power had a very clear shape. Kings sat on thrones high above everyone else, and ordinary people served them. The Roman emperor lived in a palace far away, and his soldiers and governors made sure everyone obeyed his orders. If you were a ruler, you gave commands, and other people did the hard work. Nobody questioned this. It was simply how life worked.

In that world, leaders did not serve the people. The people served the leaders. The greater you were, the more others carried your load, cooked your food, and bowed when you walked by. Power meant being lifted up while everyone else stayed down.

Then Jesus came, and He turned this whole picture upside down. He told His followers, "Those who are considered rulers of the Gentiles lord it over them... But it shall not be so among you." Those words — "it shall not be so among you" — changed everything. In God's kingdom, greatness does not come from sitting on top. It comes from kneeling down to help. The greatest person in the room is the one who serves everyone else.

This still speaks to us today, because the old way of thinking is still alive. We chase titles, promotions, and the chance to be in charge. We want people to notice us and thank us. But Jesus calls us to a different road.

Servant leadership begins with one simple question: "How can I help?" At home, it looks like doing the dishes without being asked. At work, it looks like helping a coworker finish a hard job. At church, it looks like greeting new faces and stacking chairs after the service. You do not need a title to serve. You just need a willing heart. That is where true greatness begins.

Prayer:

Father in heaven, thank You for showing us what true power looks like — Jesus, who came to serve instead of to be served. Forgive us for the times we chased titles and praise instead of looking for ways to help. Teach us to lead the way Jesus led. At home, help us do the dishes without being asked. At work, help us pitch in when a coworker is buried under a hard job. Give us eyes to notice the needs around us and hands that are quick to help. May our homes, our workplaces, and our churches be filled with the spirit of service. In Jesus' name we pray. Amen.
