Week 4 · The Cultural Context of Ancient Leadership

Thursday – Strength That Kneels

"Do your little bit of good where you are; it's those little bits of good put together that overwhelm the world." — Desmond Tutu
Bible (ESV): Philippians 2:5-8 — "Have this mind among yourselves, which is yours in Christ Jesus, who, though he was in the form of God, did not count equality with God a thing to be grasped, but emptied himself, by taking the form of a servant, being born in the likeness of men. And being found in human form, he humbled himself by becoming obedient to the point of death, even death on a cross."

The Roman Empire ruled the ancient world with force. Its power came from armies, swords, and fear. The emperor called himself lord, and his soldiers nailed his enemies to crosses along the roads as a warning to everyone else. In that world, strength meant crushing anyone who stood in your way. The strong took. The weak suffered. That was power.

Then came Jesus — and He was stronger than any emperor. The Bible tells us that Jesus was "in the form of God." He had all the power of heaven. But He "did not count equality with God a thing to be grasped." Instead, "he emptied himself by taking the form of a servant." The King of heaven became a servant. And then He went even lower: "he humbled himself by becoming obedient to the point of death — even death on a cross!" The cross, Rome's cruelest weapon, became God's greatest act of love.

This is what real strength looks like. Real strength does not crush people. It carries them. Real strength does not demand to be served. It kneels down to serve. Desmond Tutu said, "Do your little bit of good where you are; it's those little bits of good put together that overwhelm the world." Jesus proved this true. His one life of service, given all the way to the cross, changed the world forever.

We can follow His example in small ways every day. Stand up for someone who is being treated unfairly. Listen to a friend who is hurting. Do the hard, hidden work that nobody thanks you for. Strength that kneels is stronger than strength that demands. When we serve with the heart of Jesus, our little bits of good join His great work — and the world begins to change.

Prayer:

Almighty God, we stand in awe of Jesus, who had all the power of heaven yet chose the path of a servant — obedient all the way to the cross. Forgive us for using our strength to push others down instead of lifting them up. Teach us what real strength looks like: strength that kneels, strength that carries, strength that loves. Give us courage to stand up for someone treated unfairly, and humility to do the hard, hidden work nobody thanks us for. Help us trust that our little bits of good, joined to Your great work, can change the world. In Jesus' mighty name we pray. Amen.
