Week 3 · Secure Identity as a Prerequisite for Service

Tuesday – Kindness Without Keeping Score

"There are three ways to ultimate success: The first way is to be kind. The second way is to be kind. The third way is to be kind." — Fred Rogers
Bible (ESV): "For am I now seeking the approval of man, or of God? Or am I trying to please man? If I were still trying to please man, I would not be a servant of Christ." — Galatians 1:10

Have you ever done something kind, and then felt hurt when nobody thanked you? Have you ever helped someone and watched them thank everyone but you? If we are honest, most of us have felt that sting. That hurt feeling is a quiet sign that we were serving for applause, not from love. There is nothing wrong with the act — but the reason behind it was shaky.

Fred Rogers spent his whole life being kind — to children, to neighbors, to strangers. He said the way to true success is simple: be kind, be kind, be kind. He did not say "be famous." He did not say "be praised." Kindness itself is the win. And kindness only stays sweet when you do not need something back for it.

Paul understood this deeply. He wrote, "For am I now seeking the approval of man, or of God? Or am I trying to please man? If I were still trying to please man, I would not be a servant of Christ." Paul is drawing a clear line. You cannot serve two masters. If your heart is chasing people's approval, every act of service becomes a performance. You will smile while you help, but inside you will be counting who noticed and who did not.

But when your identity is secure — when you already know that God loves you and is pleased with you — you are free. You can help set up chairs, wash dishes, or visit a lonely neighbor, and it does not matter if anyone claps. You are not working for human masters. Your audience is One, and He already spoke His approval over your life.

So here is a small challenge for this week: do one kind thing that no one will ever know about. Do not tell anyone. Do not post about it. Then notice how it feels — not because someone praised you, but because your heart knows you did it for God. That quiet joy is what secure serving feels like.

Prayer:

Lord, I confess that too often I serve so people will notice me. Forgive me for chasing applause instead of chasing You. Teach my heart to rest in Your approval alone. When I set up the chairs or wash the dishes and nobody says thank you, help me smile anyway — because You saw it, and Your smile is enough. Make me kind three times over: in my words, in my actions, and in my thoughts. This week, help me do one kind thing that no one will ever know about, and let the quiet joy of it remind me that my audience is One. In Jesus' name, amen.
