Week 4 · The Cultural Context of Ancient Leadership

Friday – The Towel and the Basin

"What counts in life is not the mere fact that we have lived. It is what difference we have made to the lives of others that will determine the significance of the life we lead." — Nelson Mandela (spoken at the 90th birthday celebration of Walter Sisulu, May 18, 2002)
Bible (ESV): John 13:14-15 — "If I then, your Lord and Teacher, have washed your feet, you also ought to wash one another's feet. For I have given you an example, that you also should do just as I have done to you."

This week we have traveled back two thousand years to see how the ancient world understood power. Kings ruled from thrones. Leaders collected honor like treasure. Patrons gave gifts with strings attached. Empires ruled by force and fear. And into that world stepped Jesus, who turned every rule upside down: the greatest must be the servant, the humble will be lifted up, giving must be done in secret, and true strength kneels.

On the night before He died, Jesus showed us what all of this looks like in real life. He laid aside His outer garments, and taking a towel, tied it around His waist, and washed His disciples' dusty feet. Foot-washing was the lowest job in the house, the work of the lowest servant. But the Lord and Teacher did it Himself. Then He said, "I have given you an example, that you also should do just as I have done to you."

Jesus was not giving us a nice idea to think about. He was handing us a towel. Servant leadership is not a title or a technique. It is a daily choice to pick up the towel and look for feet to wash — to notice who is tired, who is hurting, who is overlooked, and to serve them with love.

Nelson Mandela said, "What counts in life is not the mere fact that we have lived. It is what difference we have made to the lives of others that will determine the significance of the life we lead." So here is our call as this week ends: choose one act of service and do it. Visit someone lonely. Help a neighbor. Forgive someone. Encourage a child. Then do it again next week. Pick up the towel, and keep picking it up. That is how servant leaders are made — and that is how the world is changed.

Prayer:

Lord Jesus, thank You for picking up the towel and showing us how to lead. You washed feet, touched the hurting, and gave Your life for us — and then You handed us the towel. We want to follow Your example. This week, show each of us one pair of feet to wash: the lonely person who needs a visit, the neighbor who needs a helping hand, the friend we need to forgive, the child who needs our time. Give us the courage to act, and fill our hearts with Your love so that serving becomes our joy, not our duty. Make us servant leaders who look like You. In Your precious name we pray. Amen.
