Week 3 · Secure Identity as a Prerequisite for Service

Friday – Secure Enough to Kneel

"He who loves his dream of a community more than the Christian community itself becomes a destroyer of the latter, even though his personal intentions may be ever so honest and earnest and sacrificial." — Dietrich Bonhoeffer, Life Together
Bible (ESV): "Jesus, knowing that the Father had given all things into his hands, and that he had come from God and was going back to God, rose from supper. He laid aside his outer garments, and taking a towel, tied it around his waist. Then he poured water into a basin and began to wash the disciples' feet and to wipe them with the towel that was wrapped around him." — John 13:3-5

On the night before He died, Jesus did something shocking. The Bible says, "Jesus, knowing that the Father had given all things into his hands, and that he had come from God and was going back to God, rose from supper. He laid aside his outer garments, and taking a towel, tied it around his waist. Then he poured water into a basin and began to wash the disciples' feet and to wipe them with the towel that was wrapped around him."

Look closely at the order of events. Because Jesus knew exactly who He was — where He came from and where He was going — He could kneel down and do the lowest job in the room. Washing feet was a servant's task, the job nobody wanted. But Jesus was not threatened by it, because His identity was completely settled. Only a person who is fully secure can take the lowest place without feeling low.

Insecure people cannot do this. They need the best seat, the loudest praise, the most important title. They may serve, but only if it makes them look good. Deep down, their service is really about them, not about the people they claim to help.

Dietrich Bonhoeffer warned about this danger in his book Life Together. He wrote that if you love your dream of a community more than the real Christian community itself, you will end up destroying it — even with the best intentions. In other words, if you love your perfect picture of people more than the actual, messy people in front of you, you will hurt them. Real service means loving real people — with their flaws, their slowness, and their differences — the way Jesus loved His disciples, even the one who would betray Him that very night.

This is the beating heart of servant leadership. Know who you are in God. Then kneel. Then love the actual people God placed in front of you. That is how Jesus served, and that is how He calls us to serve too.

Prayer:

Lord Jesus, You knew exactly who You were — where You came from and where You were going — and still You knelt to wash dirty feet. Give me that same deep security. When I am tempted to chase titles or to dream of perfect people instead of loving real ones, bring me back to the towel and the basin. Help me love the actual people in my life — the relative who gets on my nerves, the friend who let me down — the way You loved Your disciples, even the one who betrayed You that night. Make my heart so sure of Your love that I can take the lowest place with joy. In Your name, amen.
