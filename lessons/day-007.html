Week 2 · The Incarnation and Emptying (Philippians 2)

Tuesday – Greatness Through Serving

"Everybody can be great, because everybody can serve." — Martin Luther King Jr., from the sermon "The Drum Major Instinct," preached at Ebenezer Baptist Church, February 4, 1968
Bible (ESV): "For even the Son of Man came not to be served but to serve, and to give his life as a ransom for many." (Mark 10:45)

One Sunday morning in 1968, Dr. Martin Luther King Jr. stood in his church and talked about a hunger that lives in all of us: the "drum major instinct." That is the want to be out in front, to be seen and praised and called great.

King had just read the story in Mark 10, where two of Jesus' disciples, James and John, asked for the best seats in his kingdom — one at his right hand and one at his left. They wanted the top spots. And Jesus answered with words that turned greatness upside down: "But it shall not be so among you. Instead, whoever would be great among you must be your servant, and whoever would be first among you must be slave of all."

Then King said it plainly: "Everybody can be great, because everybody can serve." You do not have to have a college degree to serve. You do not have to be rich or famous or powerful to serve. You only need a heart full of grace and a soul full of love.

Think about what this means for your life. The world tells you greatness looks like a corner office, a big title, a crowd cheering your name. Jesus says greatness looks like a towel and a basin, like washing feet. It looks like giving your life — your time, your energy, your care — as a ransom for many, to set others free.

Who can be great? The teenager who spends Saturday mornings at the food bank. The coworker who patiently teaches the new hire the ropes. The neighbor who checks on the elderly woman next door. The parent who reads one more bedtime story when they are tired. Every one of them is living greatness.

But be careful about one thing: do not serve just to be seen. King warned about that too. True service does not need a spotlight. Serve because you love people, not because you want applause. The drum major instinct wants the front of the parade — but the servant of Christ finds joy at the back, helping everyone else march.

Prayer:

Father God, we all want to be great — to be first, to be seen. Take that hunger and turn it toward service. Give us hearts full of grace and souls full of love. Show us that true greatness looks like a towel and a basin, not a crowd cheering our name. Help us serve quietly: the teenager bagging groceries at the food bank on a Saturday morning, the neighbor checking in on the elderly woman across the street, the tired parent reading one more bedtime story. Forgive us when we serve just to be noticed. Teach us to love people, not applause. In Jesus' name, Amen.
