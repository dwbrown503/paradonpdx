Week 21 · The Crucifixion: Forgiving from the Cross

Thursday – When Forgiving Is Hard

"Everyone says forgiveness is a lovely idea, until they have something to forgive." — C.S. Lewis
Bible (ESV): Ephesians 4:32 — "Be kind to one another, tenderhearted, forgiving one another, as God in Christ forgave you."

C.S. Lewis was honest about something we all feel. Forgiveness sounds beautiful when we talk about it in general. We nod along in church. We agree it is a lovely idea. But then someone actually hurts us, deeply and personally, and suddenly forgiveness does not feel lovely at all. It feels impossible. Lewis knew that gap between the idea and the reality, and he named it honestly.

Jesus closed that gap on the cross. He did not just teach forgiveness as a lovely idea. He lived it in the hardest possible moment. Betrayed by a friend, denied by another, abandoned by almost everyone, beaten and crucified, He still said, "Father, forgive them." If anyone had a reason to say forgiveness was too hard, it was Jesus. Instead, He made it real.

Paul tells us in Ephesians 4:32 to be kind, tenderhearted, and forgiving, "as God in Christ forgave you." Notice the order. Kindness comes first. Before we can forgive the big hurts, we practice being kind in small moments. Tenderheartedness comes next. We let our hearts stay soft instead of hardening them with bitterness. Then forgiveness flows more naturally, because it grows out of a kind and tender heart.

So how do we become better people when forgiving feels hard? We start where we are. We admit to God that it is hard. We ask Him to make our hearts kind and tender. We take one small step: a prayer for the person, a refusal to speak badly about them, a decision not to replay the hurt today. We do not have to do it perfectly. We just have to start. Jesus will meet us in the trying.

Prayer:

Lord, everyone says forgiveness is lovely, until I actually have something to forgive. Today it is hard: the wound is deep, the person is unrepentant, my heart is tender. Make me tenderhearted anyway, kind anyway, forgiving anyway, just as You forgave me. When the pain flares at dinner or in the quiet night, wrap Your arms around my aching heart and soothe it with Your presence. I pray for everyone for whom forgiving feels impossible today. Do the impossible in us. Amen.
