Week 9 · Touching the Untouchable (Leper Cleansing)

Monday – Going Where It Hurts

"Compassion asks us to go where it hurts, to enter into the places of pain, to share in brokenness, fear, confusion, and anguish." — Henri Nouwen
Bible (ESV): Mark 1:41 — "Moved with pity, he stretched out his hand and touched him and said to him, 'I will; be clean.'"

In Jesus' day, a man with leprosy was not just sick. He was cast out. He could not live with his family. He could not worship with his friends. He had to shout "unclean" so people would stay away from him. He was the one everyone avoided. Then Jesus did what no one else would do. Jesus stretched out his hand and touched him. That touch is the heart of this whole story. Jesus did not shout a command from a safe distance. He did not send a helper in his place. He touched a man that the whole town would not touch. Think about the people in your own life that others step around. Maybe it is the neighbor whose yard is a mess because he is too old to care for it. Maybe it is the coworker who smells different, or the stranger on the bus who talks to himself, or the church volunteer who cries during every service and makes people uncomfortable. Compassion does not watch pain from the sidewalk. It walks across the street. It sits down next to the hurt. Going where it hurts does not mean you have to fix everything. You may not be able to heal anyone the way Jesus did. But you can do what Jesus did first. You can get close. You can offer your time, your listening ear, your kind words, your simple presence. That is what true servant leadership looks like. It leaves the comfortable spot and moves toward the hurting person. When we do that, we show people that their pain matters to us, and we show the world a picture of Jesus himself.

Prayer:

Lord, thank You for not staying far away when we were hurting. You came close and touched us with Your love. Today, give me the courage to go where it hurts, to sit beside the grieving widow, to visit the sick neighbor in the hospital, to listen to the coworker going through a divorce. Help me enter into the pain of others instead of running from it, even when it is messy. Use my hands and my time to bring Your comfort. Teach me to love the outcast, the lonely, and the sick the way You do. Amen.
