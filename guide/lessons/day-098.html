Week 20 · The Trials: Silence Under False Accusation

Wednesday – Dropping the Poison

"Resentment is like drinking poison and then hoping it will kill your enemies." — Nelson Mandela
Bible (ESV): Romans 12:19 — "Beloved, never avenge yourselves, but leave it to the wrath of God, for it is written, 'Vengeance is mine, I will repay, says the Lord.'"

Nelson Mandela spent 27 years in prison because of injustice. If anyone had a reason for resentment, it was him. Yet when he walked free, he chose not to seek revenge. He understood something powerful: holding on to resentment is like drinking poison and hoping your enemy dies. The only person it destroys is you.

Jesus lived this truth during His trials. He was beaten by soldiers, mocked by crowds, and condemned by leaders who knew He was innocent. He had every reason to be bitter. But there was no bitterness in Him. On the cross He would pray, "Father, forgive them." He refused to drink the poison.

Resentment is sneaky. It starts as a small hurt. We replay what someone did. We imagine what we wish we had said. Slowly the hurt hardens into a grudge, and the grudge starts to poison everything: our sleep, our joy, our relationships, even our health. We think we are punishing the person who hurt us, but they have moved on with their life. We are the ones still drinking the poison.

God gives us a better way in Romans 12:19. We do not have to pretend the hurt did not happen. We do not have to say it was okay. We simply hand the judgment over to God and say, "This is Yours now, not mine." That is not weakness. That is wisdom. It sets us free from carrying a weight we were never meant to carry, and it makes us kinder, lighter people to be around.

Prayer:

Lord, I have been drinking poison, replaying the hurt, rehearsing what I would say, hoping they feel my pain. But the poison only kills me. Today, help me set the cup down. I leave vengeance to You and choose to let it go. When the bitter thoughts return tonight, remind me I already dropped them. I pray for everyone poisoned by resentment. Heal them. Fill the empty space where anger lived with Your peace, and teach my heart to rest. Amen.
