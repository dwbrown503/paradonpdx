Week 16 · Inhabiting Truth and Grace

Monday – Look at What They Suffer

"We must learn to regard people less in the light of what they do or omit to do, and more in the light of what they suffer." — Dietrich Bonhoeffer
Bible (ESV): John 1:14 — "And the Word became flesh and dwelt among us, and we have seen his glory, glory as of the only Son from the Father, full of grace and truth."

Bonhoeffer wrote these words from a Nazi prison cell, where he was locked up for standing against Hitler. He had every reason to judge the people around him. Instead, he learned to look deeper. He stopped asking only, "What did this person do wrong?" and started asking, "What has this person suffered?" That one change turned judgment into compassion.

This is what it means to be full of grace and truth, like Jesus in John 1:14. Truth means we do not pretend wrong is right. Grace means we do not crush people for their wrong. Jesus lived both perfectly. He told the truth about sin, but He always saw the suffering behind the sin. He saw the lonely woman at the well, not just her broken past. He saw the hated tax collector up the tree, not just his greed.

Servant leaders need this double vision. When a volunteer lets you down, truth says the job still matters. Grace says maybe they are carrying a burden you cannot see. When a team member is difficult, truth says the behavior needs to change. Grace says lead with kindness first and find out what hurts underneath.

This calls us to be better people because everyone around you is fighting a battle you know nothing about. The rude cashier may have just lost someone she loves. The grumpy coworker may be scared about money. When you look at what people suffer instead of only what they do, your heart softens. You correct gently. You forgive quickly. You become the kind of person others feel safe around, and that is what a servant leader should be.

Prayer:

Father, the rude cashier, the angry driver, the critical relative, it is so easy to judge what they do. Today, teach me to look deeper, at what they suffer. Maybe she is grieving, maybe he is scared, maybe they are carrying a weight I cannot see. Give me eyes of compassion instead of judgment. When someone's sharp words sting me, help me wonder about their hidden pain. I pray for every hurting person acting out of their wounds today. Heal them, Lord, and soften my heart toward them. Amen.
