Week 10 · Elevating Women and Children in Ancient Culture

Monday – "Let Them Come"

"Look for the helpers. You will always find people who are helping." — Fred Rogers
Bible (ESV): Mark 10:14 — "'Let the children come to me; do not hinder them, for to such belongs the kingdom of God.'"

Picture this. Moms and dads are carrying their little ones to Jesus. They want Him to touch their kids and bless them. It is a sweet moment. But the disciples step in and push the families away. They tell them to stop. In their minds, Jesus is too important and too busy for children.

How often do we do the same thing? We are setting up chairs before church and a child wants to help. We say, "Not now, go play." We are brewing coffee and a teenager asks a long question, and we hurry them along because we have a task to finish. A brand-new volunteer stands alone in the corner and nobody walks over to say hello. We do not mean to be unkind. We are just busy. We are just focused. We are just tired.

But Jesus was not calm about this. The Bible says He was indignant. That means He was truly upset. Then He said, "Let the children come to me, and do not hinder them, for to such belongs the kingdom of God."

Jesus was saying something huge. The people the world calls small are the ones the kingdom belongs to. Children are not in the way. They are the way.

So what does this look like for us? It looks like stopping when a child talks to you and looking them in the eye. It looks like inviting the quiet volunteer to help set up the tea table and showing them where things go. It looks like saving a seat for the single mom who slips in late with a toddler on her hip, because she matters and she needs to know it.

When we welcome the smallest person in the room, we are doing exactly what Jesus did. Our actions say what His heart said: come. You belong here.

Prayer:

God, You see every person the world walks past. Help me see them too. Today, slow me down when I get too busy to care. Show me the child who needs my time, the shy new volunteer standing alone, the neighbor nobody visits. Teach me to make room the way Jesus made room, with open arms and no rushing. Let my hands and my words carry Your heart: come, you belong here. Thank You for loving me that way first, long before I deserved it. Keep shaping me into someone who opens the door instead of blocking it. Amen.
