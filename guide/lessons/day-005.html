Week 1 · The Prophetic Profile (Isaiah's Servant Songs)

Friday – The Servant Who Looks Like Jesus

"Do not waste time bothering whether you 'love' your neighbor; act as if you did. As soon as we do this we find one of the great secrets. When you are behaving as if you loved someone, you will presently come to love him." — C.S. Lewis, Mere Christianity
Bible (ESV): "For even the Son of Man came not to be served but to serve, and to give his life as a ransom for many." — Mark 10:45

All week we have looked at Isaiah's four Servant Songs: the gentle Servant who will not break a bruised reed, the called Servant who becomes a light to the nations, the steady Servant who sets his face like flint, and the suffering Servant who carries our pain. Every one of these pictures points to Jesus. Jesus said it plainly in Mark 10:45: "For even the Son of Man came not to be served but to serve, and to give his life as a ransom for many." The King of kings came as a servant. He washed His disciples' feet. He touched people no one else would touch. And He gave His life on the cross to set us free. C.S. Lewis wrote that we should not waste time asking whether we feel love for our neighbor — we should simply act as if we do. When we behave as if we love someone, we will soon come to truly love them. That is such good news. You do not have to wait until you feel like serving. Just start acting in love — help, listen, give, forgive — and your heart will follow. Servant leadership is not a title you earn. It is a way of living. It means choosing gentleness over harshness, purpose over comfort, steadiness over quitting, and other people's good over your own ease. This week, pick one person and serve them the way Jesus serves you. Make the meal. Write the note. Say the prayer. Offer the ride. Then watch what God does. You were made to be a servant leader, and the world needs the light you carry.

Prayer:

Dear God, thank You for this whole week — for the gentle Servant, the called Servant, the steady Servant, and the suffering Servant. Thank You most of all for Jesus, who did not come to be served but to serve, and gave His life for us. We do not always feel love, but teach us to act in love anyway — to make the meal, write the note, say the prayer, give the ride — and watch our hearts follow. Tonight at the kitchen table, show each of us one person to serve this week, and give us the joy of doing it. Make our whole lives look like Yours. We love You. In Jesus' name, Amen.
