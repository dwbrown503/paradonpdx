Week 26 · The Early Church Paradigm (Acts and the Epistles)

Thursday – Look at Others With Love

"We must learn to regard people less in the light of what they do or omit to do, and more in the light of what they suffer." — Dietrich Bonhoeffer
Bible (ESV): Philippians 2:3-4 — "Do nothing from selfish ambition or conceit, but in humility count others more significant than yourselves. Let each of you look not only to his own interests, but also to the interests of others."

Paul wrote to the church in Philippi with a simple but hard command: stop thinking only about yourself. Do not act from selfish ambition. Do not try to look better than others. Instead, in humility, count others more significant than yourself. That means treating other people as more important than you are. It means asking, "What do they need?" before asking, "What do I want?" This was the heartbeat of the early church. They did not just believe in Jesus. They put each other first, like Jesus put us first.

Dietrich Bonhoeffer, a pastor who stood up for what was right during a dark time in history, said we should look at people not by what they do wrong or right, but by what they suffer. That is a powerful way to serve. When someone is rude to you, it is easy to judge them. But what if you asked, "What are they going through?" Maybe they are scared. Maybe they are hurting. When you look at people through the light of their suffering, your heart softens, and serving them becomes natural.

This week, practice counting others more significant than yourself. Let someone else go first. Listen longer than you talk. When someone bothers you, pause and wonder what weight they might be carrying. Serving others in love does not mean you matter less. It means you love more. And that kind of love — the kind that puts others first — is exactly what Jesus showed us on the cross. Let His love change the way you see every person you meet today.

Prayer:

Father, forgive me for thinking of myself first, my wants, my plans, my pride. Today, teach me humility: to value others above myself, to look at their interests, not just mine. In traffic, let someone merge. In line, let someone go first. At home, listen before demanding. Give me new eyes to see people's suffering, not just their faults. Soften my heart toward the difficult people in my life. Fill me with Your love so I can look to the interests of others. Amen.
