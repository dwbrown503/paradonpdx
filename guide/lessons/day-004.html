Week 1 · The Prophetic Profile (Isaiah's Servant Songs)

Thursday – Carrying the Pain of Others

"What counts in life is not the mere fact that we have lived. It is what difference we have made to the lives of others that will determine the significance of the life we lead." — Nelson Mandela (90th birthday celebration of Walter Sisulu, May 18, 2002)
Bible (ESV): "But he was pierced for our transgressions; he was crushed for our iniquities; upon him was the chastisement that brought us peace, and with his wounds we are healed. All we like sheep have gone astray; we have turned—every one—to his own way; and the LORD has laid on him the iniquity of us all." — Isaiah 53:5–6

The fourth Servant Song, in Isaiah 52:13 through chapter 53, is the most moving picture of all. Here the Servant does something shocking: He takes our place. "He was pierced for our transgressions, he was crushed for our iniquities; upon him was the chastisement that brought us peace, and with his wounds we are healed." Transgressions are the wrong things we do. Iniquities are our sins and broken ways. The Servant did nothing wrong, yet He carried the pain that belonged to us. That is the heart of servant leadership: carrying the weight so someone else does not have to. Nelson Mandela, who spent twenty-seven years in prison for fighting against the injustice of apartheid in South Africa, said that what makes a life count is the difference we make in the lives of others. Mandela gave up his freedom so others could be free. In a smaller way, we can do the same every day. When you sit with a friend who is grieving and simply listen, you are carrying part of their sadness. When you help a coworker who is drowning in work, you are lifting their load. When you stand up for someone who is being picked on, you are taking a little of their pain onto yourself. It will cost you time and comfort. But that is exactly what Jesus did for us — and so much more. He carried our sins all the way to the cross. When we carry one another's burdens, we show the world what Jesus looks like. This week, look for someone with a heavy load, and help them carry it.

Prayer:

Dear Lord, thank You that Jesus was pierced for our wrongs and crushed for our sins — that by His wounds we are healed. He carried the weight so we would not have to. Teach us to carry weight for others in our everyday lives. When a friend is grieving, give us the grace to sit quietly beside them and listen, even when we do not know what to say. When a coworker is drowning in work, help us step in and lift some of the load. It will cost us time and comfort — but that is what love does. Make our lives count by the difference we make for others. In Jesus' name, Amen.
