Week 26 · The Early Church Paradigm (Acts and the Epistles)

Tuesday – Nobody Left Unseen

"Life's most persistent and urgent question is, 'What are you doing for others?'" — Martin Luther King Jr.
Bible (ESV): Acts 6:2-4 — "And the twelve summoned the full number of the disciples and said, “It is not right that we should give up preaching the word of God to serve tables. Therefore, brothers, pick out from among you seven men of good repute, full of the Spirit and of wisdom, whom we will appoint to this duty. But we will devote ourselves to prayer and to the ministry of the word.”"

The early church grew fast, and a problem came up: some widows were being missed when food was handed out each day. Widows in that time had no one to care for them, so being missed meant going hungry. The believers did not shrug and say, "Not our problem." They noticed. The apostles asked the church to choose seven trusted men, full of God's Spirit, to make sure every widow was fed. Serving tables may sound like a small job, but the church treated it as holy work. Nobody was left unseen.

Dr. King said the most important question in life is, "What are you doing for others?" The early church answered that question with action. They did not just care about preaching. They cared about dinner. They cared that the quiet, forgotten people were being looked after. That is what servant leadership looks like: you pay attention to the people others overlook, and you make sure their needs are met.

Who are the "widows" in your world — the people who might be missed? Maybe it is the new kid at school eating alone. Maybe it is an older neighbor who never gets visitors. Maybe it is a coworker who looks tired and quiet. You do not need a big title to serve them. You just need to notice. Ask God to open your eyes today. Then do something small and kind. When nobody is left unseen, everyone knows they belong. That is the kind of family God wants His church to be.

Prayer:

Lord, when the widows were overlooked, the church stopped everything to make sure no one was forgotten. Today, give me eyes for the unseen: the quiet kid in the back, the widow eating alone, the new family nobody greeted, the coworker everyone ignores. Help me answer the question "What are you doing for others?" with action, not just good intentions. I pray for everyone feeling invisible tonight. Let them be seen, known, and cared for. Make me someone who notices. Amen.
