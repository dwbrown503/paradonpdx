Week 18 · The Upper Room and the Basin (John 13)

Monday – Greatness Wears a Towel

"Everybody can be great because everybody can serve." — Martin Luther King Jr.
Bible (ESV): John 13:14-15 — "If I then, your Lord and Teacher, have washed your feet, you also ought to wash one another's feet. For I have given you an example, that you also should do just as I have done to you."

Picture the scene. It is the night before Jesus will die. He knows everything that is coming. And what does He do? He gets up from the table, wraps a towel around His waist, pours water into a basin, and starts washing His friends' dirty feet. In that culture, foot-washing was the job of the lowest servant in the house. Nobody with any status would do it. Yet Jesus, the Lord and Teacher, chose the lowest job on purpose.

This is what servant leadership looks like. It is not a title or a position. It is a towel. It is choosing the work nobody else wants to do and doing it with love. In real life, that might look like staying late to clean up after an event when everyone else has gone home. It might look like doing the dishes without being asked, or helping a coworker finish a hard task even though it is not your job. It might look like listening to someone who is lonely when you are tired and want to go to bed.

Here is the part that calls us to be better: Jesus did not wash feet to look good. Nobody was watching except His friends, and one of them would betray Him that very night. He served anyway. That means we serve even when nobody thanks us. We serve even the people who are hard to love. When we pick up the towel, we are following Jesus in the most direct way possible. Greatness is not about being noticed. It is about noticing who needs help and quietly helping them.

Prayer:

Lord Jesus, You wrapped a towel around Your waist and washed dusty feet, the job of the lowest servant. Today, give me the same willingness. When the dishes pile up, when the bathroom needs cleaning, when the unglamorous job falls to me, help me do it with joy instead of grumbling. Forgive my hunger for important titles. I pray for everyone doing hidden, lowly work today. Honor them. Teach me that greatness wears a towel, and let me wear mine gladly. Amen.
