Week 21 · The Crucifixion: Forgiving from the Cross

Tuesday – The Power to Forgive

"We must develop and maintain the capacity to forgive. He who is devoid of the power to forgive is devoid of the power to love." — Martin Luther King Jr.
Bible (ESV): Colossians 3:13 — "bearing with one another and, if one has a complaint against another, forgiving each other; as the Lord has forgiven you, so you also must forgive."

Dr. King said something striking: if you cannot forgive, you cannot truly love. Forgiveness is not just a nice extra in the Christian life. It is the engine that makes love possible. Without it, every relationship eventually breaks down, because every person you love will hurt you at some point. Husbands and wives, parents and children, friends, coworkers — all of them will let you down. The only question is whether you will forgive.

Jesus forgave from the cross, and He calls us to do the same. Paul writes that we must forgive each other "as the Lord has forgiven you." Think about how much God has forgiven you. Every wrong thing you have ever done, every selfish choice, every unkind word — all forgiven because of Jesus. When we remember how much we have been forgiven, forgiving others becomes not just possible but natural. How can we hold a small debt against someone when God cancelled our enormous one?

Developing the capacity to forgive is like building a muscle. It gets stronger with practice. Start with small things. Forgive the driver who cut you off. Forgive the friend who forgot your birthday. Forgive the family member whose comment stung. Each small act of forgiveness builds your capacity for the bigger ones.

This calls us to be better people because unforgiveness poisons us from the inside. A person who cannot forgive becomes bitter, suspicious, and lonely. A person who forgives becomes free, warm, and full of love. Choose forgiveness, and you choose love. Choose love, and you look like Jesus.

Prayer:

Lord, I feel powerless to forgive. The complaint keeps replaying, the irritation keeps rising. But You say forgiveness is power, and its absence leaves me powerless. Today, fill me with Your power to release the coworker who wronged me, the friend who disappointed me. Help me bear with others the way You bear with me. I pray for everyone who feels devoid of the power to forgive. Empower them. Let my forgiving heart prove that Your strength lives in me. Amen.
