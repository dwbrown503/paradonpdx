Week 19 · Gethsemane: Submission to the Father's Will

Tuesday – The Painful Best

"We are not necessarily doubting that God will do the best for us; we are wondering how painful the best will turn out to be." — C.S. Lewis
Bible (ESV): Luke 22:42 — "saying, 'Father, if you are willing, remove this cup from me. Nevertheless, not my will, but yours, be done.'"

Jesus knew the Father's plan was the best plan. He never doubted that. What made Him sweat drops like blood in the garden was knowing how painful that best plan would be. The cross was coming, and He could feel it. C.S. Lewis put words to the feeling many of us know: we trust God, but we are honest enough to wonder how much it is going to hurt.

This is one of the most human moments in the whole Bible. Jesus did not pretend the cup was easy to drink. He asked, "Father, if you are willing, remove this cup from me." He was honest about His pain. And then He said the words that changed everything: "Nevertheless, not my will, but yours, be done." He chose the Father's will over His own comfort.

Submission does not mean we enjoy the pain. It means we trust the Person who allows it. Think of a parent who holds a child still for a painful shot at the doctor. The child does not understand, and it hurts. But the parent knows the shot will protect the child from something worse. In the same way, God sometimes leads us through painful things because He sees what we cannot see.

How does this call us to be better people? First, it teaches us to be honest with God. We can tell Him we are scared. Second, it teaches us to trust Him anyway. And third, it makes us gentle with others who are suffering. Instead of judging someone who is struggling to obey God, we can remember Gethsemane and say, "I understand. This is hard. But God is good, and He will carry you through."

Prayer:

Father, I believe You want the best for me, but honestly, I am wondering how painful the best will turn out to be. The diagnosis, the job loss, the broken dream: they hurt. Like Jesus praying "not my will, but Yours," I surrender my plans today. When the cup does not pass, hold my hand through drinking it. I pray for everyone facing a painful best today. Give us grace to trust You in the dark. Turn our tears into testimony. Amen.
