Week 1 · The Prophetic Profile (Isaiah's Servant Songs)

Wednesday – Steady When the Road Is Hard

"We must learn to regard people less in the light of what they do or omit to do, and more in the light of what they suffer." — Dietrich Bonhoeffer, Letters and Papers from Prison
Bible (ESV): "I gave my back to those who strike, and my cheeks to those who pull out the beard; I hid not my face from disgrace and spitting. But the Lord GOD helps me; therefore I have not been disgraced; therefore I have set my face like a flint, and I know that I shall not be put to shame." — Isaiah 50:6–7

The third Servant Song, in Isaiah 50, shows us a Servant who faces terrible treatment. People beat his back, pulled out his beard, mocked him, and spit in his face. Yet he did not run away or give up. He said, "Because the Sovereign LORD helps me, I will not be disgraced. I have set my face like flint." Flint is a very hard stone. Setting your face like flint means deciding, deep inside, that you will keep going no matter what. Dietrich Bonhoeffer, a pastor who stood up for what was right even when it cost him his life, said we must see people less by what they do wrong and more by what they suffer. In other words, a servant leader looks at people's pain before judging their mistakes. This matters so much. When someone is rude to you, it is easy to write them off. But a servant leader asks, "What is this person going through?" Maybe the grumpy coworker just lost a loved one. Maybe the angry teenager is scared inside. Serving people is not always easy or fun. Sometimes the people you help do not thank you. Sometimes doing the right thing costs you time, money, or comfort. The Servant shows us that we can stay steady because God helps us. You do not have to be strong on your own. When serving feels hard, lean on God's strength. Set your face like flint, keep your heart soft, and keep going. That is what servant leaders do.

Prayer:

Dear God, thank You for the Servant who set His face like flint and did not turn back, even when people hurt Him. Give us that same steady courage. When a coworker is rude to us at the start of a hard day, help us wonder what pain they are carrying instead of writing them off. When we serve and nobody says thank you, be our strength so we do not quit. Teach us to look at others through their suffering, not just their mistakes. Keep our hearts soft and our feet steady, and help us keep going — because You help us, and You never leave us alone. In Jesus' name, Amen.
