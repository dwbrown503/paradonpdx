Week 19 · Gethsemane: Submission to the Father's Will

Monday – Love Deeper Than the Pit

"There is no pit so deep that God's love is not deeper still." — Corrie ten Boom
Bible (ESV): Hebrews 5:7 — "In the days of his flesh, Jesus offered up prayers and supplications, with loud cries and tears, to him who was able to save him from death, and he was heard because of his reverence."

The Garden of Gethsemane was Jesus' darkest night. He knew the cross was coming. The Bible says His soul was very sorrowful, even to death, and He prayed with loud cries and tears. If anyone has ever been in a pit, it was Jesus that night. He felt the full weight of fear, sadness, and dread.

But here is the beautiful truth Corrie ten Boom learned in her own suffering: there is no pit so deep that God's love is not deeper still. Jesus was heard, the verse says, because of His reverence. He did not get rescued from the cross. He got something better: the nearness of the Father in the middle of it. An angel came and strengthened Him. God did not remove the cup, but God never left Him alone in the garden.

This matters for us because we all visit dark gardens. Maybe yours is a hospital waiting room, a broken relationship, a season of depression, or a worry that keeps you up at night. In those moments it can feel like God is far away. But the story of Gethsemane tells us He is closer than ever. His love goes deeper than your deepest pit.

A servant leader does not pretend to have no fears. Jesus Himself cried and trembled. What makes Him our example is that He brought His fears to the Father instead of running away. When we do the same, we find strength to keep going, and we become people who can sit with others in their dark gardens too. We can say with honesty, "I know what it feels like. And I know God is still here."

Prayer:

Lord, I am in a pit right now, grief, fear, pain that will not lift. But Your love is deeper still. Today, when I cannot feel You, help me trust You. Like Jesus in Gethsemane, I bring You my loud cries and my tears; hold them gently. I pray for everyone in their own deep pit tonight, the hospital room, the empty house, the anxious mind. Reach down with Your deeper love. Remind us that no pit is too deep for You. Amen.
