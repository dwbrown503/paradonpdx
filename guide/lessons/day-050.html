Week 10 · Elevating Women and Children in Ancient Culture

Friday – "All One in Christ"

"Do all the good you can, by all the means you can, in all the ways you can, in all the places you can, at all the times you can, to all the people you can, as long as ever you can." — John Wesley
Bible (ESV): Galatians 3:28 — "There is neither Jew nor Greek, there is neither slave nor free, there is no male and female, for you are all one in Christ Jesus."

In the ancient world, people were divided into boxes. Jew or Gentile. Slave or free. Male or female. Your box decided your worth. It decided where you could go, what you could learn, and who would listen to you. Women and children sat at the very bottom of the ladder. Slaves were treated like property. These walls were thick and tall.

Then Jesus came, and He kept crossing the lines. He talked with the Samaritan woman at the well when no respectable man would. He let Mary sit at His feet like a disciple. He touched lepers. He welcomed children when His own followers tried to stop them. He ate with tax collectors. Again and again, Jesus treated pushed-aside people as full people.

Paul sums it up in Galatians 3:28. In Christ, the boxes break. There is neither Jew nor Greek, neither slave nor free, no male and female. We are all one in Christ Jesus. This does not mean our differences disappear. It means our differences no longer decide our worth. In God's family, nobody is a second-class citizen.

What does this look like today? It looks like men and women serving side by side on the same team, both valued, both heard. It looks like treating the person who cleans the building with the same respect we give the pastor. It looks like a child being prayed for like their prayers count, because they do. It looks like inviting the new family, the single dad, the teenager, the quiet older woman to the table, not the kids' table, the real table.

John Wesley said to do all the good we can to all the people we can. That is only possible when we stop ranking people. When we see every person as one with us in Christ, kindness stops being charity from above and becomes family caring for family.

This is the heart of servant leadership. The leader does not stand on a higher step. The leader kneels to lift others up, because in Christ there is no higher step. We are all one.

Prayer:

Lord, thank You that in Christ there is no "us" and "them," no favorites, no outsiders. Today, help me treat everyone the same: the rich and the poor, the longtime member and the first-time guest, the boss and the janitor. Forgive me for the times I played favorites or made someone feel like they did not belong. Open my eyes to the walls I still build. Make my home, my workplace, and my church a place where every person is welcomed, valued, and loved equally. Amen.
