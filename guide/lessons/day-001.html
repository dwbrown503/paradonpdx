Week 1 · The Prophetic Profile (Isaiah's Servant Songs)

Monday – Greatness Comes Through Serving

"Everybody can be great…because anybody can serve. You don't have to have a college degree to serve. You don't have to make your subject and verb agree to serve. You only need a heart full of grace. A soul generated by love." — Martin Luther King Jr., from his sermon "The Drum Major Instinct" (Ebenezer Baptist Church, February 4, 1968)
Bible (ESV): "Behold my servant, whom I uphold, my chosen, in whom my soul delights; I have put my Spirit upon him; he will bring forth justice to the nations. ... A bruised reed he will not break, and a faintly burning wick he will not quench; he will faithfully bring forth justice." — Isaiah 42:1, 3

Long before Jesus was born, the prophet Isaiah painted a picture of God's chosen Servant. Notice something surprising: this Servant does not lead by shouting or pushing people around. He does not break the bruised reed — a reed that is cracked and weak — and he does not quench a candle wick that is barely still burning. That means he is gentle with people who are hurting and almost out of strength. Martin Luther King Jr. said that anyone can be great because anyone can serve. You do not need a fancy degree or a big title. You only need a heart full of grace and a soul filled with love. Think about what this looks like in real life. At home, it means speaking kindly to a tired spouse or a frustrated child instead of snapping back. At work, it means helping a new coworker who keeps making mistakes instead of rolling your eyes. At church or in your neighborhood, it means noticing the person who sits alone and going to sit with them. Serving people gently does not mean being weak. The Servant in Isaiah is strong enough to bring justice to the whole world, but he chooses to be tender with the wounded. That is the kind of leader God honors. This week, ask yourself: who is a "bruised reed" in my life — someone bending under pressure? How can I help them stand tall again instead of pushing them down? When you choose gentleness over harshness, you are leading the way Isaiah's Servant leads, and the way Jesus leads too.

Prayer:

Dear God, You are so gentle with us when we are weak — You never break the bruised reed or snuff out the small flame. Make our hearts soft like Yours. Tomorrow morning, when our child is cranky and slow getting ready for school, help us speak kindly instead of snapping. At work, when a coworker messes up again, keep us from rolling our eyes and teach us to help instead. Show us the bruised reeds in our lives — the people bending under pressure — and give us tender hands to lift them up. Make us leaders who help people stand tall again. In Jesus' name, Amen.
