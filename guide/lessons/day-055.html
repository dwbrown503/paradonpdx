Week 11 · Crossing Racial Boundaries (The Samaritan Woman)

Friday – "Power Used for Good"

"Power at its best is love implementing the demands of justice." — Martin Luther King Jr.
Bible (ESV): Micah 6:8 — "He has told you, O man, what is good; and what does the LORD require of you but to do justice, and to love kindness, and to walk humbly with your God?"

Power is a gift, and it can be used in two ways. Power can crush, or power can lift. A boss can use his power to bully, or to protect. A teacher can use her power to shame, or to build up. A nation can use its power to conquer, or to serve. History is full of people who used power to push others down — often along racial lines, deciding that some people were worth less.

Jesus had all power. He could have called down angels. He could have made kings bow. Instead, He washed feet, touched people with leprosy, welcomed children, and spoke with a Samaritan woman that respectable people avoided. His power always moved toward the lowly, never away from them.

Martin Luther King Jr. said, "Power at its best is love implementing the demands of justice." Power at its worst is selfishness dressed up as strength. Power at its best looks like love with its sleeves rolled up — love doing the hard work of making things right.

Micah 6:8 gives us three simple instructions, and they fit on an index card: act justly, love mercy, and walk humbly with God.

Act justly means doing what is right even when it costs you. Speak up when someone is treated unfairly because of their race. Share opportunities with people who are usually left out. Don't stay neutral.

Love mercy means leading with kindness. Forgive quickly. Give people a second chance. Remember that everyone you meet is fighting a battle you cannot see.

Walk humbly with God means remembering you are not better than anyone. The moment we think we are above another person because of our skin, our money, or our status, we have forgotten who we are. We are all loved by the same God.

Here is the beautiful part: when you use your power this way, you become a bridge. Divided people start to trust each other, wounds start to heal, and the world gets a small picture of heaven, where every nation stands together before God. That is what servant leadership is for.

Prayer:

God, You have placed influence in my hands, at work, at home, in my community. Teach me to use my power the way You use Yours: to lift up, not push down. Today, when I have a choice between what is easy and what is right, help me choose what is right. Show me how to do justice, love kindness, and walk humbly. I pray for leaders everywhere, bosses, teachers, parents, pastors. Turn our power into love that serves. Make my strength a shelter for the weak, not a weapon against them. Amen.
