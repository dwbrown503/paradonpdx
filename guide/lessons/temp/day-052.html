Week 11 · Crossing Racial Boundaries (The Samaritan Woman)

Tuesday – "The Neighbor on the Road"

"If you are neutral in situations of injustice, you have chosen the side of the oppressor." — Desmond Tutu
Bible (ESV): Luke 10:33-34 — "'But a Samaritan, as he journeyed, came to where he was, and when he saw him, he had compassion. He went to him and bound up his wounds, pouring on oil and wine.'"

In Luke 10, Jesus tells a story about a man beaten and left half dead on the road. Two good, religious men walk right past him. A priest crosses to the other side. A temple worker does the same. They see the suffering, and they keep walking. They stay neutral.

Then a Samaritan comes down the road. Here is what made this story shock everyone listening: Jews and Samaritans were enemies. They hated each other over race and religion. The hurt man would have expected the Samaritan to laugh at him or walk on by. Instead, the Samaritan stops. He takes pity on the man. He bandages his wounds with his own hands. He pours oil and wine on them to ease the pain. He lifts the stranger onto his own donkey, takes him to an inn, and pays for his care.

The lesson is clear. Love is not just a feeling you keep in your heart. Love is what you do with your hands and your feet. And love crosses the lines that hate draws.

Our quote today is strong: "If you are neutral in situations of injustice, you have chosen the side of the oppressor." The priest and the temple worker chose a side when they walked past. Doing nothing was a choice.

We face the same choice. When a coworker is treated unfairly because of the color of their skin, we can speak up or look away. When a neighbor from another country is mocked, we can stand beside them or stay silent. When a rule at work or school hurts one group more than others, we can ask questions or shrug.

The Samaritan shows us the way. Notice the small steps: he saw, he felt pity, he went to him. Seeing comes first. Most of us never get close enough to see. Slow down. Look around. Find the person on the roadside in your world — the new kid, the lonely coworker, the family that just moved in — and be the neighbor who stops.

Prayer:

Lord, thank You for the Good Samaritan, who stopped when everyone else walked by. Today, do not let me be the one who crosses to the other side. When I see someone stranded, hurting, or in trouble, on the road, at work, in my neighborhood, help me stop, help, and stay until they are okay. Forgive me for the times I stayed neutral and called it wisdom. Teach me that love always takes a side: the side of the hurting. Make me a neighbor to whoever You place in my path today. Amen.
