Week 13 · Rebuking Religious Exploitation (Cleansing the Temple)

Friday – For All Nations

"It is in your hands to make of our world a better one for all." — Nelson Mandela
Bible (ESV): Mark 11:17 — "And he was teaching them and saying to them, 'Is it not written, "My house shall be called a house of prayer for all the nations"? But you have made it a den of robbers.'"

When Jesus cleansed the temple, He quoted God's promise: "My house will be called a house of prayer for all nations." For all nations. Not just for one group. Not just for the rich, the important, or the people who look and talk like us. For everyone. Every nation, every background, every kind of person is welcome in God's house.

But the sellers and money changers had set up their tables in the outer court — the very place where people from all nations came to pray. Their greed was blocking the doorway. Imagine traveling far to meet God, only to find the entrance crowded with shouting sellers and unfair prices. That is what made Jesus so bold that day. He was clearing the way so that everyone could come.

This is a powerful picture of what servant leadership looks like. Servant leaders clear the way for others. They remove the barriers that keep people out. They make room. They say, "You belong here," to the person who feels like they don't.

Nelson Mandela said, "It is in your hands to make of our world a better one for all." Notice: for all. Not just for people like us. Making the world better means opening doors, not closing them. It means learning someone's name, hearing their story, and treating them with dignity — no matter where they come from or what they have been through.

Ask yourself: are there people I keep at a distance? Someone from a different background, a different neighborhood, a different story? This week, take one step closer. Smile. Start a conversation. Offer help. Clear a path the way Jesus did.

God's house is for all nations — and so is God's love. Let's live like we believe it.

Prayer:

Lord, Your house shall be called a house of prayer for all nations. Today, widen my heart to match Yours. Forgive me for the times I made church, or my table, or my friendship, feel like it was only for people like me. Help me welcome the stranger, the immigrant, the person whose story I do not understand yet. I pray for every nation, every tribe, every tongue. Bring us together under Your roof. Make my life a doorway, never a wall, for all the peoples You love. Amen.
