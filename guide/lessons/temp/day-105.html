Week 21 · The Crucifixion: Forgiving from the Cross

Friday – Love That Changes Enemies

"Love is the only force capable of transforming an enemy into a friend." — Martin Luther King Jr.
Bible (ESV): Romans 5:8 — "but God shows his love for us in that while we were still sinners, Christ died for us."

Dr. King lived this truth. He was hated, threatened, and attacked for standing up for justice. He could have answered hate with hate. Instead, he insisted that only love can transform an enemy into a friend. He learned this from Jesus, who died for the very people who were killing Him. While we were still sinners, still God's enemies, Christ died for us. That is the most stunning love in history.

Think about what happened after the cross. One of the soldiers who stood at the foot of the cross watched Jesus die and said, "Truly this was the Son of God." An enemy became a believer. Love did what force never could. It changed a heart.

This is how servant leaders change the world. Not by defeating their enemies, but by loving them. That does not mean we approve of wrongdoing or pretend evil is good. It means we refuse to dehumanize the people who oppose us. We see them as people God loves. We pray for them. We treat them with dignity. We look for chances to do good to them.

How does this call us to be better people? It challenges us to love the difficult people in our lives: the rude neighbor, the harsh boss, the family member who always criticizes, the person who believes the opposite of everything we believe. We do not have to agree with them. We do have to love them. And when we do, something miraculous can happen. Not always, but sometimes, love transforms an enemy into a friend. Even when it does not change them, it changes us. We become more like Jesus, who loved His enemies to the very end.

Prayer:

God, only love can turn an enemy into a friend, and You proved it by dying for me while I was still Your enemy. Today, help me love the person who feels like my enemy: the hostile neighbor, the rival at work, the family member who chose the other side. Bless them genuinely. Pray for them sincerely. I pray for every bitter feud tonight, in homes, in workplaces, in nations. Send Your transforming love. Let my changed heart be the first miracle. Amen.
