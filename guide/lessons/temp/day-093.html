Week 19 · Gethsemane: Submission to the Father's Will

Wednesday – One Step in the Dark

"Faith is taking the first step even when you don't see the whole staircase." — Martin Luther King Jr.
Bible (ESV): Matthew 26:39 — "And going a little farther he fell on his face and prayed, saying, 'My Father, if it be possible, let this cup pass from me; nevertheless, not as I will, but as you will.'"

In Gethsemane, Jesus could not see past the cross. He knew the suffering that was coming, but the resurrection was still hidden from His human eyes in that dark moment. He did not have the whole staircase in view. He only had the next step: pray, surrender, and trust. So He took it. He fell on His face, prayed honestly, and said, "Nevertheless, not as I will, but as you will."

That is what faith looks like. Faith is not having all the answers. It is not seeing the whole plan laid out like a map. Faith is taking the first step when you can only see one step. Martin Luther King Jr. knew this well. When he marched for justice, he could not see how it would all turn out. He just took the next faithful step, and then the next.

Most of us want the whole staircase. We want God to show us how everything will work out before we obey. We want to know the ending before we take the first step. But God usually gives us just enough light for the step we are on. He asks us to trust Him with the rest.

This calls us to be better people in two ways. First, we learn to obey God one step at a time, even when we are afraid. Maybe that step is forgiving someone, or telling the truth, or volunteering when you feel unqualified. Second, we become encouragers to others who are stuck. When someone is frozen with fear about the future, we can gently say, "You don't have to see the whole staircase. Just take the first step. God will show you the next one."

Prayer:

God, I cannot see the whole staircase, just the next dark step. A move, a hard conversation, a new beginning: it frightens me. Today, give me faith for one step. Like Jesus walking toward the cross in the dark, help me walk toward what You ask, trusting You see what I cannot. When my imagination runs wild with worst cases, quiet it with Your presence. I pray for everyone frozen at the edge of a dark step today. Take our hands. Walk us through, one step at a time. Amen.
