Week 26 · The Early Church Paradigm (Acts and the Epistles)

Monday – They Shared Everything They Had

"The best way to find yourself is to lose yourself in the service of others." — Mahatma Gandhi
Bible (ESV): Acts 2:44-47 — "And all who believed were together and had all things in common. And they were selling their possessions and belongings and distributing the proceeds to all, as any had need. And day by day, attending the temple together and breaking bread in their homes, they received their food with glad and generous hearts, praising God and having favor with all the people. And the Lord added to their number day by day those who were being saved."

The very first church was a family that shared. When the believers came to faith in Jesus, they did not hold tight to what was "mine." They saw a brother or sister in need, and they sold what they had to help. Nobody was forced to do it. Their hearts were full of Jesus, so their hands opened wide. They ate together, prayed together, and made sure nobody went without. That is a beautiful picture of what serving looks like: noticing a need and filling it with what you have.

This is not just about money. Gandhi said we find ourselves when we lose ourselves in serving others, and that is exactly what the first believers did. They stopped living only for themselves and started living for each other. When we share what we have — our time, our food, our help — we discover a joy that keeping everything for ourselves could never give. Real life is not found in collecting. It is found in giving.

Today, you can live like the early church. Look around your home, your school, or your work. Who needs help? Maybe a neighbor needs a meal. Maybe a friend needs a ride. Maybe someone just needs your time. Sharing what you have does not make you poor. It makes you rich in the way that matters most. When you serve others freely, people see God's love in you, just like the world saw it in that first church. Be a sharer, not a keeper, and watch how your heart grows.

Prayer:

God, the early church shared everything. No one claimed their things were only their own. Today, loosen my grip on "mine." The extra bedroom, the second car, the pantry full of food: show me who needs what I have. Help me share without keeping score and give without announcing it. Forgive my clutching and my storing up. I pray for every needy person tonight. Move Your people to share. Make my home a little outpost of Your generous kingdom. Amen.
