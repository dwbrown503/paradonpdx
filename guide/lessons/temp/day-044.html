Week 9 · Touching the Untouchable (Leper Cleansing)

Thursday – Rolling Up Our Sleeves

"Life's most persistent and urgent question is, 'What are you doing for others?'" — Martin Luther King Jr.
Bible (ESV): James 1:27 — "Religion that is pure and undefiled before God the Father is this: to visit orphans and widows in their affliction, and to keep oneself unstained from the world."

It is easy to say we care about people. It is harder to roll up our sleeves and show it. The Bible tells us that real faith is not just what we believe in our heads or what we say with our mouths. Real faith is what we do with our hands. Visiting orphans and widows in their affliction means moving toward the people who have the least help and giving them our time and our strength. That is what Jesus did for the man with leprosy. He did not just feel sorry for him from far away. He reached out and touched him. His love had hands and feet. We can do the same in our everyday lives. Rolling up our sleeves might look like bringing a warm meal to the widow who lives alone on your street. It might look like helping the single dad next door fix his broken fence on a Saturday morning. It might look like sitting with a lonely elderly man at church instead of rushing home after service. It might look like giving a ride to the coworker whose car broke down, even when it makes your day longer. These are not big, famous acts. They are small, faithful ones. But they answer the most urgent question of all. What are you doing for others? A servant leader does not wait to be asked. He looks for the need and steps in. When we serve the forgotten and the hurting, we are practicing the purest kind of faith there is, the kind God smiles on. Our sleeves may get dirty. Our plans may get interrupted. But our hearts will grow, and the people we serve will know they are loved.

Prayer:

Father God, thank You for showing me that real faith is not just words, but actions. Forgive me for the times I said I cared but did nothing. Today, open my eyes to the needs around me, the lonely widow, the struggling single parent, the forgotten neighbor, and give me willing hands and an open schedule to serve them with joy. Help me answer the question "What are you doing for others?" with my life, not just my words. Make me a servant leader who sees the need and steps in with love. Amen.
