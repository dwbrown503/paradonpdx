Week 18 · The Upper Room and the Basin (John 13)

Wednesday – Looking Up, Not Down

"A proud man is always looking down on things and people; and, of course, as long as you are looking down, you cannot see something that is above you." — C.S. Lewis
Bible (ESV): Philippians 2:3-5 — "Do nothing from selfish ambition or conceit, but in humility count others more significant than yourselves. Let each of you look not only to his own interests, but also to the interests of others. Have this mind among yourselves, which is yours in Christ Jesus,"

Pride has a way of bending our necks downward. When we think we are better than others, we start looking down on them. We notice their mistakes. We feel annoyed by their weaknesses. We keep score of how much we do compared to everyone else. C.S. Lewis warned that as long as we are looking down, we cannot see what is above us. We miss God, and we miss the good in other people.

Jesus did the opposite in the upper room. He was above everyone in that room. He was the Son of God. He had every right to be served. Instead, He got down low. He counted His disciples more significant than Himself. He looked not only to His own interests, but also to theirs. That is the mind Paul tells us to have: the same mind that is ours in Christ Jesus.

What does this look like in real life? It looks like letting someone else go first, even when you were there first. It looks like admitting you were wrong instead of defending yourself. It looks like asking a quiet person for their opinion because their thoughts matter too. It looks like doing a task well even when someone with less experience gets the credit.

Counting others more significant than yourself does not mean thinking you are worthless. It means thinking of yourself less and thinking of others more. When we stop looking down on people, we start seeing them the way God sees them: precious, loved, and worth serving. That is how we become better people to those around us, one humble choice at a time.

Prayer:

Father, forgive my proud heart. Too often I look down on people, the slow cashier, the struggling student, the person who does not think like me, and while I am looking down, I cannot see You. Today, lift my eyes. Help me count others as more important than myself, the way Jesus did. When pride whispers that I am better, silence it with humility. I pray for everyone crushed by someone's contempt today. Lift their heads. Teach me to look up to You and across at others with love. Amen.
