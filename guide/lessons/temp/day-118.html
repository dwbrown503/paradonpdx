Week 24 · Restoring the Fallen Leader (Peter on the Beach)

Wednesday – Feed My Sheep

"Life's most persistent and urgent question is, 'What are you doing for others?'" — Martin Luther King Jr.
Bible (ESV): John 21:16-17 — "He said to him a second time, ‘Simon, son of John, do you love me?’ He said to him, ‘Yes, Lord; you know that I love you.’ He said to him, ‘Tend my sheep.’ He said to him the third time, ‘Simon, son of John, do you love me?’ Peter was grieved because he said to him the third time, ‘Do you love me?’ and he said to him, ‘Lord, you know everything; you know that I love you.’ Jesus said to him, ‘Feed my sheep.’"

After every one of Peter's answers, Jesus gave him a job. "Feed my lambs." "Tend my sheep." "Feed my sheep." Stop and think about how amazing that is. Peter had just failed in the biggest way. The natural thing would be for Jesus to say, "You are forgiven — now sit down and be quiet for a while." But Jesus did the opposite. He put Peter back to work. He trusted him with the most precious thing Jesus had: His people.

That is the deepest sign of forgiveness. Anyone can say the words "I forgive you." But when you hand someone a real job again, when you trust them with something that matters, that is forgiveness with muscle. Jesus was saying, "I don't just wipe your slate clean. I still believe in you. I still need you."

Martin Luther King Jr. once said that life's most persistent and urgent question is, "What are you doing for others?" Jesus answered that question for Peter on the beach. What Peter would do for others was this: feed them, care for them, lead them. Restoration is not about going back to the couch. Restoration is about getting back to serving.

This matters for how we treat people who have failed and come back. When someone has truly turned around, we do them no favor by keeping them on the sidelines forever. At some point, love says, "I trust you again. Here is something important for you to do." Serving others is part of healing. The fastest way to feel whole again is to pour yourself out for someone else. So if you have failed, hear Jesus' voice: your story is not over. There are sheep who need feeding, and He is asking you to feed them.

Prayer:

Lord, You did not just forgive Peter, You gave him a job: feed My sheep. Today, give me my assignment too. Show me who You have entrusted to me: my children, my team at work, the new believer, the lonely neighbor. Help me feed them, with truth, with encouragement, with practical help, with love. When I feel unqualified, remind me that love qualifies me. I pray for every shepherd of a small flock today. Strengthen our hands for the feeding. Amen.
