Week 17 · Forgiveness as a Leadership Tool

Thursday – Faithful in Small Things

"Be faithful in small things because it is in them that your strength lies." — Mother Teresa
Bible (ESV): Matthew 18:21-22 — "Then Peter came up and said to him, ‘Lord, how often will my brother sin against me, and I forgive him? As many as seven times?’ Jesus said to him, ‘I do not say to you seven times, but seventy-seven times.’"

Peter thought he was being generous. The religious teachers of his day said you only had to forgive three times. Peter doubled it and added one more: seven times! He probably expected Jesus to be impressed. Instead, Jesus said seventy-seven times. He was not giving Peter a math problem. He was saying forgiveness is not something you count. It is something you live, over and over, in the small moments of every day.

Mother Teresa understood this. She said our strength lies in being faithful in small things. Forgiveness is mostly a small thing. It is not usually about forgiving a prison guard like Corrie ten Boom did. It is about forgiving the small, daily hurts: the rude comment, the forgotten promise, the interrupted sentence, the credit someone else took for your idea. These little unforgiven moments pile up like small stones in your shoe. Each one is tiny, but together they make every step painful.

A servant leader who practices small forgiveness stays strong. When you let go of the little hurts quickly, they never grow into big bitterness. Your heart stays light. Your team stays healthy. People around you feel the difference because you are not carrying yesterday's stones into today's work.

This calls us to be better people because strength is built in the small, unseen choices. Nobody will applaud you for forgiving a rude comment. Nobody will notice when you let go of a small hurt. But God notices, and your heart knows. Be faithful in the small forgivenesses today, and you will find you have strength for the big ones when they come.

Prayer:

Lord, forgiving once is hard, and forgiving seventy times seven feels impossible. Yet in my marriage, my family, my friendships, the small hurts keep coming. Today, give me strength for the small things: the forgotten promise, the sharp tone, the thoughtless word. Help me forgive quickly and completely, before bitterness takes root. I pray for everyone keeping count of wrongs today. Teach us to lose count and keep loving. Show me that faithfulness in small forgiving is where real strength lives. Amen.
