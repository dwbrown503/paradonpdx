Week 2 · The Incarnation and Emptying (Philippians 2)

Wednesday – Living for Others

"The church is the church only when it exists for others." — Dietrich Bonhoeffer, Letters and Papers from Prison (written from his prison cell, 1944)
Bible (ESV): "Do nothing from selfish ambition or conceit, but in humility count others more significant than yourselves. Let each of you look not only to his own interests, but also to the interests of others." (Philippians 2:3–4)

Dietrich Bonhoeffer was a pastor in Germany during the dark days of World War II. He watched many churches close their doors to the suffering around them. They cared about protecting themselves — their buildings, their image, their safety. Bonhoeffer was thrown into prison for standing up for what was right. And from his prison cell, he wrote these powerful words: "The church is the church only when it exists for others."

Read that again slowly. A church is only truly a church when it lives for others, not for itself. The same is true for every leader, every family, every team. We are not at our best when we exist to serve ourselves. We are at our best when we exist for others.

Paul wrote the same truth long before Bonhoeffer: "In humility count others more significant than yourselves." That does not mean you are worthless — far from it. You are made in God's image and deeply loved. It means you choose to put the good of others ahead of your own comfort. "Let each of you look not only to his own interests, but also to the interests of others."

What does that look like in real life? It looks like the boss who asks her team, "What do you need to do your best work?" It looks like the basketball player who passes the ball to the open teammate instead of forcing a bad shot for his own glory. It looks like the friend who puts down the phone and listens — really listens — instead of waiting for a turn to talk. It looks like a church that opens its doors to feed hungry neighbors instead of only feeding itself.

Selfish ambition says, "What can I get?" Conceit says, "Look at me." But the servant leader says, "What do you need?" and "How can I help?" That one small change in the question changes everything. Try it this week: in every conversation, in every decision, ask how it can serve the other person. You will be amazed at what God does through a life lived for others.

Prayer:

Lord, forgive us for living only for ourselves. Forgive our selfish ambition and our need to be noticed. Teach us to put others first the way You did. At work, help us ask, "What do you need to do your best?" instead of looking out for ourselves. At home, help us put down our phones and really listen to the people we love, instead of waiting for our turn to talk. Give us Bonhoeffer's courage to stand with those who suffer. Make our homes, our teams, and our churches places that exist for others — every single day. Amen.
