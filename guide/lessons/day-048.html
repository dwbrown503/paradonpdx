Wednesday – "Act Like Love"

"Do not waste time bothering whether you 'love' your neighbor; act as if you did." — C.S. Lewis
Bible (ESV): Matthew 25:40 — "'Truly, I say to you, as you did it to one of the least of these my brothers, you did it to me.'"

In Matthew 25, Jesus paints a picture of the final day. The King looks at His people and says something shocking. When you fed the hungry, you fed Me. When you gave water to the thirsty, you gave it to Me. When you visited the lonely and the prisoner, you visited Me. His people ask, "When did we do all that for You?" And He answers, "As you did it to one of the least of these my brothers, you did it to me."

The "least of these" are the people the world overlooks. The hungry neighbor. The single mom stretching one paycheck. The unhoused man sleeping under the bridge. The quiet kid nobody picks. Jesus says serving them is the same as serving Him. Not like serving Him. It IS serving Him.

Here is the honest part. We do not always feel love for every neighbor. Some people are hard to love. They annoy us. They disagree with us. They take our time. C.S. Lewis gives us the way forward: do not sit around wondering if you feel love for them. Act as if you did. Bring the meal anyway. Help carry the groceries anyway. Sit and listen anyway.

Feelings often follow actions. When we serve with our hands, our hearts start to catch up. The volunteer who signs up to serve coffee out of duty finds himself learning the regulars' names and caring about their week. The person who visits the lonely neighbor "because it's the right thing" finds she looks forward to the visit. Love grows in the soil of action.

And Jesus promises something beautiful. On the last day, all those small, hidden acts of kindness will be named by the King Himself. Nothing done in love is wasted. The sandwich handed to a hungry stranger, the ride given to the elderly woman, the kind word to the cashier — Jesus counts every one as done for Him.

So today, pick one small act. Do it for someone who cannot pay you back. That is what love looks like when it puts on work boots.

Prayer:

Lord Jesus, open my eyes to the least of these around me, the hungry, the lonely, the overlooked, the ones nobody thanks. When I do not feel love toward my difficult neighbor or my critical coworker, help me act in love anyway: hold the door, offer help, speak kindly. Let my heart catch up to my hands. Thank You for loving me when I was hard to love. Teach me that love is not just a feeling. It is a choice I make with my feet and my hands, one small act at a time. Amen.
