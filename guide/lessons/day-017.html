Week 4 · The Cultural Context of Ancient Leadership

Tuesday – The Honor Game

"He will not be thinking about humility: he will not be thinking about himself at all." — C.S. Lewis (from Mere Christianity, in the chapter "The Great Sin")
Bible (ESV): Luke 14:11 — "For everyone who exalts himself will be humbled, and he who humbles himself will be exalted." Mark 12:38-39 — "And in his teaching he said, 'Beware of the scribes, who like to walk around in long robes and like greetings in the marketplaces and have the best seats in the synagogues and the places of honor at feasts.'"

In the ancient world, honor was like money. It was the most valuable thing a person could own. Leaders worked hard to collect it. They wore special robes so everyone would notice them. They loved being greeted with respect in the marketplace. At meals, they fought for the best seats, the places closest to the host. Every title, every greeting, every seat showed the world how important they were.

Jesus watched all of this, and He warned His followers not to play the honor game. He said, "Watch out for the teachers of the law," because they loved the robes, the greetings, and the best seats. Then He gave the rule that turns honor upside down: "All those who exalt themselves will be humbled, and those who humble themselves will be exalted." If you lift yourself up, God will bring you down. But if you lower yourself to serve, God will lift you up.

This is where C.S. Lewis helps us. He said that a truly humble person is not even thinking about himself at all. He is too busy taking a real interest in other people. The proud person is always asking, "How do I look? Did they notice me? Did I get the credit?" The humble person is asking, "How are you? What do you need? How can I help?"

We still play the honor game today. We want the credit. We want our name first on the list. We want people to know how hard we worked. But a servant leader steps out of the spotlight and lets others shine. Let someone else take the best seat. Praise a coworker in front of others. Do good work and let God notice. When we stop chasing honor, we find something better: the quiet joy of lifting others up.

Prayer:

Lord Jesus, You watched the leaders of Your day chase honor — the robes, the greetings, the best seats — and You showed us a better way. Forgive us for playing the honor game: for wanting our name first on the list, for bringing up our hard work in meetings so people will notice. Teach us the quiet joy of stepping out of the spotlight. Help us let someone else take the best seat, and praise a coworker in front of others without needing any of it back. When we are tempted to lift ourselves up, remind us of Your promise: You lift up the humble. We want to look like You. In Your name we pray. Amen.
