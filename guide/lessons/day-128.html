Week 26 · The Early Church Paradigm (Acts and the Epistles)

Wednesday – Shepherds, Not Kings

"Do small things with great love." — Mother Teresa
Bible (ESV): 1 Peter 5:2-3 — "shepherd the flock of God that is among you, exercising oversight, not under compulsion, but willingly, as God would have you; not for shameful gain, but eagerly; not domineering over those in your charge, but being examples to the flock."

In the early church, leaders were called shepherds, not kings. A shepherd does not boss the sheep around. A shepherd feeds them, protects them, and walks with them. Peter wrote that leaders should serve because they are willing, not because they have to. They should not lead for money or power. They should lead by example, showing others how to love and serve. That is a very different picture of leadership than the world gives us. The world says leaders get the biggest office and the most praise. God says leaders get the towel and the washbasin.

Mother Teresa understood this. She served the poorest of the poor in Calcutta, doing small things — cleaning wounds, holding hands, feeding the hungry — with great love. She did not need a big title. Her love was the title. Real leaders do not ask, "How can people serve me?" They ask, "How can I serve people?" When you are willing to do the small jobs nobody sees, you become the kind of leader others want to follow.

You may not be a pastor or a boss, but you are a leader somewhere. Maybe you are the oldest child at home. Maybe friends look up to you. Maybe you lead a team at work. Lead like a shepherd. Do the small things with great love. Help without being asked. Admit when you are wrong. Put others first. People will remember your kindness far longer than your words. A leader who serves is a leader who lasts, because love is the strongest power there is.

Prayer:

God, You call leaders to shepherd, not to rule, to serve willingly, not for gain or power. Today, when I lead at work, at home, or at church, keep me from bossing and bullying. Help me guide with a willing heart: the quiet help, the patient listening, the encouraging word, the humble apology. When power tempts me, remind me of the Good Shepherd who laid down His life. I pray for every leader tonight. Make us shepherds with Your heart. Amen.
