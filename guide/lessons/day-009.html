Week 2 · The Incarnation and Emptying (Philippians 2)

Thursday – A Life That Lifts Others

"What counts in life is not the mere fact that we have lived. It is what difference we have made to the lives of others that will determine the significance of the life we lead." — Nelson Mandela, at the 90th birthday celebration of Walter Sisulu, Johannesburg, May 18, 2002
Bible (ESV): "Have this mind among yourselves, which is yours in Christ Jesus, who, though he was in the form of God, did not count equality with God a thing to be grasped, but emptied himself, by taking the form of a servant, being born in the likeness of men. And being found in human form, he humbled himself by becoming obedient to the point of death, even death on a cross." (Philippians 2:5–8)

Nelson Mandela spent 27 years in prison. He lost his freedom, his family time, his youth. When he finally walked out, he could have come out full of hate. Instead, he chose to lift up a whole nation. He said, "What counts in life is not the mere fact that we have lived. It is what difference we have made to the lives of others that will determine the significance of the life we lead."

Stop and think about that. One day, each of us will leave this world. The question will not be how long we lived, how much money we made, or how many awards we won. The question will be: what difference did we make to the lives of others? That is the true measure of a life.

Jesus is the perfect picture of this. Paul tells us to have "the same mind as Christ Jesus." What was his mind? He was in the form of God, yet he did not count equality with God a thing to be grasped. He emptied himself. He became a servant. He humbled himself to the point of death on a cross. Every step down was a step toward lifting us up. His whole life was difference-making.

A servant leader measures success the same way. Not by how many people follow them, but by how many people they lift. Not by how high they climb, but by how many they help climb.

Look around you. The teacher who stays late so a struggling student can finally pass. The father who works long hours so his children can go to school. The leader who opens doors for others instead of walking through them alone. These are significant lives.

This week, ask yourself one question each morning: "Whose life will be better today because of me?" Then go and do one thing — one kind, brave, humble thing — that makes the answer real. That is how a life becomes significant. Not by living for yourself, but by pouring yourself out for others, just as Christ did for you.

Prayer:

God of mercy, thank You for Jesus, who poured Himself out all the way to the cross so we could be lifted up. Teach us to measure our lives the way You do — not by what we gain, but by the difference we make for others. Forgive us for chasing things that do not last. Give us Mandela's heart, choosing love over hate, and Christ's heart, choosing the cross over comfort. Each morning, help us ask, "Whose life will be better today because of me?" — then give us the courage to do one kind, brave, humble thing that makes the answer real. May our lives lift others, to Your glory. Amen.
