Week 11 · Crossing Racial Boundaries (The Samaritan Woman)

Wednesday – "No Favorites With God"

"I never ran my train off the track and I never lost a passenger." — Harriet Tubman
Bible (ESV): Acts 10:34-35 — "'Truly I understand that God shows no partiality, but in every nation anyone who fears him and does what is right is acceptable to him.'"

Peter was a good man, but he had a blind spot. He believed God's love was mostly for Jewish people like him. Then one day God gave him a vision — a sheet full of animals he had been taught were unclean. A voice said, "Do not call anything impure that God has made clean." Peter was confused. Then a knock came at the door. A Roman soldier named Cornelius, a Gentile, had sent for him. Romans were the enemy. Gentiles were outsiders. But Peter went. He walked into Cornelius's house, shared the good news of Jesus, and watched the Holy Spirit fall on everyone there — Jew and Gentile alike. That is when Peter spoke the words of our verse: God does not show favoritism. He accepts people from every nation.

Now think of Harriet Tubman. Born into slavery, she escaped to freedom — and then went back, again and again, to lead others out. She said, "I never ran my train off the track and I never lost a passenger." Every life mattered to her. She risked everything for people the world said did not matter. She treated each person as equally precious, because to her they were.

This is what it means that God has no favorites. He does not rank people by skin color, language, or where they were born. In His eyes, the manager and the janitor stand on level ground. The immigrant family and the family that has lived here for generations are equally loved.

Our job as servant leaders is to treat people the same way. That means we do not give better care to people who look like us. We do not save our kindness for our own group. We notice who gets overlooked — the quiet person in the back, the worker nobody thanks, the neighbor nobody invites — and we give them the same honor we would give anyone else. When we live with no favorites, we show the world what God is like.

Prayer:

Heavenly Father, thank You for showing no favorites. You welcome people from every nation, every background, every story. Forgive me for the quiet prejudices I carry, the assumptions I make, and the people I overlook without even thinking. Today, help me see every person as equally precious to You: the immigrant family, the homeless man, the wealthy executive. Like Harriet, help me lead others toward freedom instead of leaving anyone behind. Let my life declare that with You, no one is second-class. Amen.
