Week 24 · Restoring the Fallen Leader (Peter on the Beach)

Tuesday – Three Chances to Say Yes

"There is no pit so deep, that God's love is not deeper still." — Corrie ten Boom
Bible (ESV): John 21:15-17 — "When they had finished breakfast, Jesus said to Simon Peter, ‘Simon, son of John, do you love me more than these?’ He said to him, ‘Yes, Lord; you know that I love you.’ He said to him, ‘Feed my lambs.’ He said to him a second time, ‘Simon, son of John, do you love me?’ He said to him, ‘Yes, Lord; you know that I love you.’ He said to him, ‘Tend my sheep.’ He said to him the third time, ‘Simon, son of John, do you love me?’ Peter was grieved because he said to him the third time, ‘Do you love me?’ and he said to him, ‘Lord, you know everything; you know that I love you.’ Jesus said to him, ‘Feed my sheep.’"

Peter had said "no" to Jesus three times. He denied Him once, then again, then a third time. That number must have echoed in Peter's mind like a drumbeat. Three times. So what did Jesus do? He asked Peter a question — three times. "Do you love me? Do you love me? Do you love me?" For every "no," Jesus gave Peter a chance to say "yes." Three failures were covered by three confessions of love.

This is no accident. Jesus was gently undoing Peter's shame. He was not rubbing it in. He was healing it, one question at a time, until the pain of the denials was replaced by the joy of declaring love. Peter was hurt when Jesus asked the third time — it stung — but that sting was the sting of a wound being cleaned so it could heal.

Corrie ten Boom understood this. She survived terrible suffering in a Nazi prison camp, and she said, "There is no pit so deep, that God's love is not deeper still." No matter how far we fall, God's love goes further down and lifts us back up.

Here is what this means for us. When someone has failed, they do not need us to keep reminding them of the failure. They need us to ask them, with love, "Are you still in this? Do you still love what is good? Do you still want to serve?" We give them the chance to answer yes again — not once, but as many times as it takes for their heart to believe it. And when someone around us fails, we can tell them the truth of this lesson: your lowest moment is not deeper than God's love. Say yes to Him again. He will meet your answer with open arms, every time.

Prayer:

Father, Peter denied You three times, and You gave him three chances to say "I love You." Today, thank You for my second chances: the fresh morning after yesterday's failure, the restored friendship, the forgiven debt, the clean slate. When shame tells me I am disqualified, let Your voice be louder: "Do you love me? Then begin again." I pray for everyone who thinks they have blown it for good. Give them another chance. Let my restored life restore others. Amen.
